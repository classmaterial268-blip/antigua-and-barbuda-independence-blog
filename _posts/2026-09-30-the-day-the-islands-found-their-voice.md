What does independence really begin with a flag, a ceremony, or the courage of people demanding change?

On 1 November 1981, Antigua and Barbuda stepped into a new era. Independence was not simply a change in government it was a declaration that the islands would shape their own future (Government Information Service of Antigua and Barbuda, n.d.).

Before independence, Antigua and Barbuda had been connected politically to Britain. Over time, the people of the islands developed stronger demands for self-government and the right to make decisions about their own future.

A key figure in this journey was Vere Cornwall Bird. As a labour leader and later the first Prime Minister of independent Antigua and Barbuda, Bird helped give workers a stronger voice and connected their demands for fair treatment with the wider struggle for political change (CARICOM, 2020).

Independence represented national self determination and the creation of a sovereign state. It recognized the hopes, work, and resilience of generations of people.

Today, Independence Day gives citizens an opportunity to remember the past, celebrate national progress, and think about the responsibilities that come with freedom.
